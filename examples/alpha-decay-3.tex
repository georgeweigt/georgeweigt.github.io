\section*{Alpha decay 3}

From the previous section we have
\begin{equation*}
\tau_{1/2}=\frac{2\log2}{\beta}\frac{r_1}{\sqrt{E/m}}e^{2\gamma}
\end{equation*}

We can estimate $\beta$ from the following half-life data.
Rows with $Z=90$ are uranium and $Z=88$ are thorium.
Recall that $Z$ is atomic number minus two.
\begin{equation*}
\begin{matrix}
\log\tau_{1/2} & A & Z & E
\\[1ex]
6.30 & 228 & 90 & 6.80
\\
14.4 & 230 & 90 & 5.99
\\
21.5 & 232 & 90 & 5.41
\\
29.7 & 234 & 90 & 4.86
\\
34.2 & 236 & 90 & 4.57
\\
39.5 & 238 & 90 & 4.27
\\
7.52 & 226 & 88 & 6.45
\\
17.9 & 228 & 88 & 5.52
\\
28.5 & 230 & 88 & 4.77
\\
40.6 & 232 & 88 & 4.08
\end{matrix}
\end{equation*}

Take the log of both sides.
\begin{equation*}
\log\tau_{1/2}=-\log\beta+\log\left(2\log2\frac{r_1}{\sqrt{E/m}}\right)+2\gamma
\end{equation*}

Rearrange as
\begin{equation*}
\log\beta=-\log\tau_{1/2}+\log\left(2\log2\frac{r_1}{\sqrt{E/m}}\right)+2\gamma
\end{equation*}

Let $y$ be the residuals
\begin{equation*}
y=-\log\tau_{1/2}+\log\left(2\log2\frac{r_1}{\sqrt{E/m}}\right)+2\gamma
\end{equation*}

The ordinary least squares estimate of $\log\beta$ is the average of the residuals.
\begin{equation*}
\log\beta=\bar y
\end{equation*}

From the data we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\bar y=6.737
\end{equation*}

Hence
\begin{equation*}
\beta=\exp(\bar y)=843
\end{equation*}

\end{matrix}
\end{equation*}
