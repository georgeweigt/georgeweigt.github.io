\section*{Ehrenfest theorem}

The Ehrenfest theorem tells us expectation values obey the laws of classical mechanics.
For example, by the classical momentum formula $p=mv$ we have
\begin{equation*}
\langle p\rangle=m\frac{d}{dt}\langle x\rangle
\tag{1}
\end{equation*}

To verify (1), start with momentum expectation value $\langle p\rangle$.
\begin{equation*}
\langle p\rangle=\int\psi^*p\psi\,dx
\end{equation*}

By the substitution
\begin{equation*}
p=\frac{im}{\hbar}[H,x]
\end{equation*}

we have
\begin{equation*}
\langle p\rangle=\frac{im}{\hbar}\int\psi^*[H,x]\psi\,dx
\end{equation*}

Expand the commutator.
\begin{equation*}
\langle p\rangle=\frac{im}{\hbar}\int\psi^*Hx\psi\,dx-\frac{im}{\hbar}\int\psi^*xH\psi\,dx
\end{equation*}

By the Schrodinger equation we have the substitutions
\begin{equation*}
H\psi=i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\psi
\end{equation*}

and
\begin{equation*}
\psi^*H=(H\psi)^*=-i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\psi^*
\end{equation*}

Hence
\begin{equation*}
\langle p\rangle=\frac{im}{\hbar}\int-i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\psi^*x\psi\,dx
-\frac{im}{\hbar}\int\psi^*xi\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\psi\,dx
\end{equation*}

Cancel $\hbar$.
\begin{equation*}
\langle p\rangle=m\int\frac{d}{dt}\psi^*x\psi\,dx+m\int\psi^*x\frac{d}{dt}\psi\,dx
\end{equation*}

By the product rule for differentiation
\begin{equation*}
\langle p\rangle=m\frac{d}{dt}\int\psi^*x\psi\,dx
\end{equation*}

Hence
\begin{equation*}
\langle p\rangle=m\frac{d}{dt}\langle x\rangle
\end{equation*}
