\section*{Lorentz invariance}

Let $X$ be a space-like vector.
There exists a Lorentz transformation $\Lambda$ such that the
time component vanishes in the transformed vector $X'=\Lambda X$.

\bigskip
For example, let $X$ be the space-like vector
\begin{equation*}
X=\begin{pmatrix}2\\0\\0\\3\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}

Verify $X$ is space-like.
\begin{equation*}
X^\mu X_\mu=2^2-3^2=-5
\end{equation*}

We seek $\Lambda$ such that
\begin{equation*}
X'=\Lambda X=\begin{pmatrix}
\gamma & 0 & 0 & -\beta\gamma
\\
0 & 1 & 0 & 0
\\
0 & 0 & 1 & 0
\\
-\beta\gamma & 0 & 0 & \gamma
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}2\\0\\0\\3\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\0\\z'\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}

where $0\le\beta<1$ and
\begin{equation*}
\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}
\end{equation*}

This reduces to finding $\beta$ such that
\begin{equation*}
2\gamma-3\beta\gamma=0
\end{equation*}

Solve for $\beta$.
\begin{equation*}
\beta=\frac{2}{3}
\end{equation*}

Hence
\begin{equation*}
\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}
3/\sqrt5 & 0 & 0 & -2/\sqrt5
\\
0 & 1 & 0 & 0
\\
0 & 0 & 1 & 0
\\
-2/\sqrt5 & 0 & 0 & 3/\sqrt5
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}

and
\begin{equation*}
X'=\Lambda X=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\0\\\sqrt5\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
