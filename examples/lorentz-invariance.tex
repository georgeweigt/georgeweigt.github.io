\section*{Lorentz invariance}

Let $X$ be a space-like vector.
There is a $\Lambda$ such that the
time component of $\Lambda X$ vanishes.

\bigskip
For example, let $X$ be the space-like vector
\begin{equation*}
X=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\\2\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}

Verify $X$ is space-like.
\begin{equation*}
X^\mu X_\mu=-3
\end{equation*}

We seek $\Lambda$ such that
\begin{equation*}
\Lambda X=\begin{pmatrix}
\cosh\eta & 0 & 0 & \sinh\eta
\\
0 & 1 & 0 & 0
\\
0 & 0 & 1 & 0
\\
\sinh\eta & 0 & 0 & \cosh\eta
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\\2\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\0\\z\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}

This reduces to finding $\eta$ such that
\begin{equation*}
\cosh\eta+2\sinh\eta=0
\end{equation*}

Solve numerically to obtain
\begin{equation*}
\eta=-0.549306
\end{equation*}

Hence
\begin{equation*}
\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}
1.1547 & 0 & 0 & -0.57735
\\
0 & 1 & 0 & 0
\\
0 & 0 & 1 & 0
\\
-0.57735 & 0 & 0 & 1.1547
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
